# Production phrase meanings — approved reference

Snapshot: November 9, 2026. **All 90 entries approved by the user and applied to production on November 9, 2026.**

This is the approved editorial reference. See [phrase editorial standards](phrase-editorial-standards.md) when adding new phrases. The current/proposed labels below preserve the before/after comparison from review; proposed values are now the applied values.

Original AI-drafted explanations. Literary and ambiguous attributions selectively checked against the linked references. Proverb/idiom/common-saying labels are editorial classifications, not proof of earliest origin. User approval covers all 90 entries; historical origins were not exhaustively researched.

Meanings are proposed display copy. Current attribution is shown exactly as stored; (empty) means production currently has no attribution. The proposed label may classify a phrase without naming an author. Reference links are background evidence, not text to copy into the app.

For later revisions, use the stable entry numbers and retain the research notes and references.

## Priority attribution reviews

- **11. All that glitters is not gold** — Current author-only credit hides that this is a proverb, not a securely attributed original saying. Shakespeare uses glisters, not this exact modern wording. Proposed label distinguishes the proverb from the literary parallel.
- **53. Knowledge is power** — Qualified attribution only. Bacon's Latin wording is usually translated as knowledge itself is power; this shorter wording and its modern meaning should not be presented as an exact English quotation.
- **63. No man is an island** — Modernized excerpt from Donne; attribution identifies the work, not a proverb origin.
- **77. The pen is mightier than the sword** — Named attribution supported for this wording in Richelieu.
- **78. The secret of getting ahead is getting started** — Remove the unsupported Mark Twain credit. Quotation research finds anonymous early instances; authorship remains uncertain.
- **86. What doesn't kill you makes you stronger** — Modern second-person adaptation of Nietzsche's first-person aphorism, not an exact quotation. Explanation is a claim of the saying, not a guarantee about trauma.
